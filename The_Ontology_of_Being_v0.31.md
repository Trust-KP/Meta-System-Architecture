## Abstract

Every theory rests upon conditions it cannot fully explain from within itself. Continuation requires systems capable of persisting across time. Systems require distinction. Distinction requires a boundary. Pursued to its limit, each layer yields to a deeper condition, and that condition yields to another. This work records the structure found at the bottom of that descent, and then tells it forward.

Told forward, the path is single. Before a system is identified, there is a condition in which no distinction holds but the pressure toward distinction is accumulating. When that pressure can no longer be contained, a boundary forms. From boundary comes distinction, the moment at which difference holds and a system becomes possible. From distinction comes the space of what can be, and from that space one possibility is realised into existence. Existence, once achieved, must be renewed, or it ends. The system continues, or it does not.

This sequence — Indeterminacy, Boundary, Distinction, Possibility, Existence, Continuation — is the order in which a system becomes observable and identifiable to a cognitive agent, and the order of the conditions each state requires of the next. It is a human construction of the kind that holds until observation resists it. It does not claim to describe the world as it is apart from any describer; whether a given system also passes through the path in time is a claim about that system's domain, and is kept where it can be tested.

Any system that can be described is described through four dimensions: what it means (Interpretation, 释), how it is formed (Structure, 构), what governs it (Regime, 度), and what it does (Realisation, 行). The four are not imposed from outside. Each arises at one crossing of the path: structure when the boundary is crossed, interpretation when distinction is crossed, regime when possibility is shaped, and realisation when a possibility becomes existence. That is why there are four, and why a system that exists has all of them.

The four dimensions are Being: the form through which any system is described, the same for every system. The six states are State. The form does not change; the system it describes is renewed at every moment, and State unfolds. A State examined as a system becomes a Being with its own four dimensions and its own path. By this recursion the architecture extends itself without new categories.

Because one grammar describes both how systems arise and how they are observed, it can be run in either direction. It can generate a system that does not yet exist, represent one that already operates, and trace an observed system back to the conditions it required. A system generated and then traced should return to its declaration. The architecture is in this sense reversible, and the reversal is its own test.

This proposition is called the KP Conjecture: any system, across any domain, is fully expressible through the six-state path and the four dimensions. It is not a theorem. It is a constraint. To refute it is not to argue against it, but to produce a system that cannot be described within it.

## Scope and Non-Scope

**Scope.** This work is the meta view. It concerns the structure of any system as such: the path by which a system arises and persists, and the dimensions through which it can be described. It operates without committing to a domain, scale or level. Works at a particular scale or in a particular domain are realisations of it.

**Non-scope.** This work does not supply the content of any domain. It does not say which distinctions a given system should draw, which rules it should adopt, or what it should value. It does not settle questions that belong to the sciences — what selects one outcome in a physical measurement, for example — and its examples should not be read as taking sides in them. It does not claim to be the only valid description of a system; it claims that any valid description can be expressed within it.

It does not claim to describe the world as it is apart from any describer. A system, in this work, is a structured interpretation of the world made by a cognitive agent, held until observation resists it (0.7).

## Part 0 — The Descent and the Conjecture

### 0.1 The descent

This work was not begun at the beginning. It was begun in the middle of things, with systems that already continue: cooperation between people, institutions that outlast their founders, trust extended across time. Each, when asked what it relied on, pointed downward. Trust relied on continuation. Continuation relied on a system that could continue. A system relied on being distinguishable from what it was not. Distinction relied on a boundary. At the boundary the questions stopped yielding a further layer that could be described, and pointed only to a condition before description: indeterminacy.

That descent is the method. A coherent framework, pressed to explain the conditions it relies on, exposes a deeper layer. What appears as a failure to explain is a signal pointing down. The descent is made from continuation because it can only be made from there. Nothing can look back on its own origin except something that has lasted long enough to look.

What follows tells the path forward, from indeterminacy to continuation, as recovered from the descent. It is told by something that continues, about how anything comes to continue.

### 0.2 The conjecture

> **KP Conjecture.** Any system, across any domain, is fully expressible through the six-state path from Indeterminacy to Continuation, and through the four dimensions of Interpretation, Structure, Regime and Realisation.

The six states are the path a system takes:

**Indeterminacy → Boundary → Distinction → Possibility → Existence → Continuation**

The four dimensions are how any point on that path is seen:

| Interpretation 释 | Structure 构 | Regime 度 | Realisation 行 |
|---|---|---|---|
| what it means; what counts within it | how it is formed; how its parts relate | what is permitted; where its limits are | what it does; what effects it has |

The four dimensions are **Being**: the form through which any system becomes observable, the same for every system. The six states are **State**: the conditions through which any system unfolds. The form does not change; the system it describes is renewed at every moment (Part 5). Being observes. State unfolds.

### 0.3 How the four arise along the path

The four dimensions are not set beside the six states from outside. In the description of a system, each becomes available at one crossing of the middle of the path:

| Crossing | Dimension that arises | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| Boundary | 构 Structure | Crossing the boundary gives the system its first form: an inside and an outside |
| Distinction | 释 Interpretation | Crossing distinction describes the system: what it is, what it is not, what counts within it |
| Possibility | 度 Regime | Rules shape the possibility space: what is permitted determines which outcomes remain open |
| Existence | 行 Realisation | The selected possibility is enacted and has effects |

Indeterminacy lies before the four, when none has yet arisen. Continuation lies after them, when all four must be renewed together. A system that exists therefore has all four; a system that continues renews all four; a system that fails to renew any one of them degrades.

Two things follow, and both matter.

First, the order is the order of identification. It is the order in which a system becomes observable and identifiable to a cognitive agent: the dimensions become available in the order 构, 释, 度, 行. To an observer, all four can be asked of every state: one may ask what a boundary means, what governs a crossing, what a boundary does, even though at the boundary only form is given. The grid of six states and four dimensions describes; the order of arising is what the description tracks.

Second, the order does not bind the author. A designer may write a protocol's rules before any instance exists, or a contract's terms before the relationship begins. In generating a system, all four dimensions may be declared at once and in any order. The order holds for the system that results as it is identified: its structure forms when its boundary is first held, its meaning when it is first distinguished, its regime when its possibilities are first constrained, its effects when it first acts. Whether a given system also passes through these in time within its own domain is a claim about that domain, held in the register.

### 0.4 Properties

**Sufficiency.** No examined system has required a fifth dimension. This now has a reason: the four dimensions arise at the four crossings between Indeterminacy and Continuation, so a fifth dimension would require a fifth crossing, and therefore a seventh state. To refute sufficiency is to show a missing state, not merely to name a missing category.

**Order.** The six states cannot be reordered. Nothing is distinguished without a boundary; nothing is possible without distinction; nothing exists that was not possible; nothing continues that did not exist. The order is the order of identification, not of authorship. A distinction may operate in a system long before anyone describes it; describing it is an event in the describer's own path (Part 2).

**Free entry.** Any of the four dimensions may serve as the starting point of description. The underlying path is the same.

**No partial systems.** Every subsystem is itself a complete system. There are only systems that have not yet been fully described.

**Recursion.** A State examined as a system becomes a Being, with its own four dimensions and its own path. Continuation is the sixth state of the path; it is also, taken as a system, the subject of a whole body of work on how cooperative systems persist, with its own interpretation, structure, regime and realisation. The State did not cease to be a State. It was taken as a system. The same object exists at two levels of description without conflict, because the levels are distinct.

### 0.5 Three uses of one grammar

The architecture is used in three ways. They are one grammar run in different directions.

To **generate** is to run it forward: from a declared scope and purpose to a system that does not yet exist — its boundary, its distinctions, its possibilities, the events that realise it, and the rules by which it renews. To **represent** is to run it across: to place a system that already operates onto the grid of states and dimensions. To **trace** is to run it backward: from an observed system, event or failure to the state and dimension whose conditions were met, missing or broken.

The older names for the two directions are synthesis and analysis. Analysis works back from what is sought to the principles it requires; synthesis builds forward from those principles to the result. The 易传 says it of life and death: 原始反终 — trace to the beginning, return to the end. A grammar that serves both for producing and for parsing is called reversible. This architecture is reversible, and the descent of 0.1 was its first use: a trace, made from continuation, which became a generation when it was told forward.

Generation is of two kinds. It is **constitutive** when adopting what is generated makes the system: a contract, a law, a protocol. It is **representative** when what is generated describes a system that would operate anyway: a model of a market, a theory of a cell. The same grammar does both. Which one a given use is should be declared, because a constitutive generation is judged by whether the system it makes works, and a representative one by whether it fits what was already there.

Reversibility gives the architecture a test it can apply to itself. Generate a system from a declaration; let it run; trace it back without sight of the declaration. The trace should return the declaration — the same boundary, the same distinctions, the same rules of renewal. Where it does not, the state at which generation and tracing part company is where the grammar has a gap.

### 0.6 What kind of claim this is

The six states and the four dimensions are a **grammar**. A grammar is not confirmed by examples, because loose enough terms will fit anything. It is judged by whether it describes without remainder and without contradiction, and it is refuted by a case it cannot describe.

Some things the architecture says are **claims about the world**: that systems missing a dimension fail sooner than those that have all four, for example, or that boundaries form before identities. Claims of this kind are confirmed or refuted only by cases recorded before their outcome was known. The architecture keeps such claims, with the conditions under which they would fail, in a separate register.

The architecture as a whole is a projection: the expected description of any system. Use, test and the judgment of others are the actual. The difference between them is how the projection is renewed.

The four dimensions have been named in other words by other traditions, and in other words again in the works that realise this one. Different words, definitions or pairings for the same dimension are language. They are not inconsistencies, and this work does not ask other vocabularies to agree with its own.

### 0.7 Stance: a cognitive and social ontology

The six states and the four dimensions are a human construction. A system, in this work, is a structured interpretation of the world made by a cognitive agent and fixed in artifacts (a definition, a model, a rule, a ledger) so that others can use it and build on it. It holds as fact for those who use it until observation resists it. The world is taken to exist apart from any describer; what this work describes is not the world but systems as they become identifiable within it.

"Cognitive agent" is not restricted to humans: it means any agent that distinguishes, interprets and carries what it has found across time. Human systems are the case examined here.

Three consequences follow.

First, the KP Conjecture is a claim about description and design. No system can be identified, built or kept by a cognitive agent without the four dimensions, and none can be identified without passing the six states in order. It is tested against institutions that failed and those that lasted, recorded before their outcome was known (the register).

Second, the architecture is itself a system of this kind: collectively held, anchored in versioned papers, open to refutation (0.6 and Part 6).

Third, claims about experience are claims made from inside a system. They are anchored by agreement among reports, not by external measurement.

*Reading note.* In Parts 1 to 5, statements such as "a boundary forms" or "pressure accumulates" describe a system as identified. They are not claims about the physics of any domain unless the domain makes them. Passages in 1.1 and 2.1 that read as claims about the world apart from any describer are to be reworded in a later version.

## Part 1 — Indeterminacy and Boundary

### 1.1 The state

Before a system there is not nothing. There is material that is not yet separated: gradients, tendencies, pressures, differences that do not hold. This is indeterminacy. It is undivided, but it is not still. Pressure toward separation accumulates within it.

When the pressure can no longer be contained, a boundary forms. A boundary is the first separation that holds: an inside and an outside, such that something now crosses or does not cross. The boundary separates. It does not yet say what has been separated. Saying that is the work of distinction.

Indeterminacy can only be seen looking back. From inside it there is no distinction by which it could be seen. Every account of indeterminacy, including this one, is written from the far side of a boundary that has already formed.

### 1.2 Through the four dimensions

**Structure** arises here. The boundary gives the system its first form: an interior and an exterior, a shape that persists. Nothing more is yet structured; there are no parts within, only within and without.

**Interpretation**, seen from outside, asks what the boundary is a boundary *of*. In the system, nothing is yet named; to an observer, the boundary already invites the question that distinction will answer.

**Regime**, seen from outside, asks what may cross. The rules of crossing will come later, when possibilities are shaped, but they will act here, at the boundary, because that is where crossing happens. 界之所守，度也: what keeps the boundary is regime. What forms it is structure.

**Realisation**, seen from outside, is crossing itself. Every exchange — of matter, energy, money, information — is a crossing, and so every exchange presupposes a boundary.

### 1.3 Why it cannot be removed or merged

Without boundary there is no account of exchange, because there is nothing to cross. A theory that begins at distinction must explain what an exchange crosses, and cannot.

Merged into distinction, boundary would make separation and identification a single act. But systems are often separated before they are identified. A membrane closes before anything inside it could be called a cell. A circle of people accept one another's tokens before anyone names the token money. A watershed divides the rain before anyone maps it. Separation and identification are two events.

### 1.4 Two systems

**The cell.** Molecules with one end drawn to water and the other repelled by it, placed in water, close into a double layer without design. The pressure before closure is the cost of exposing the repelled ends; the closure is the boundary. Some molecules now cross; others do not. Nothing is yet a cell.

**The currency.** Before money there is barter and debt among people who happen to trade. A boundary forms when a set of people consistently accept the same token from one another and not from strangers. The circle of acceptance is the boundary. It holds before the token has a name, an issuer, or a law behind it.

Traced backward, the death of a cell is often a failure of its membrane, and the debasement of a currency is often a failure of its circle: tokens crossing in from outside without control. To generate a cell-like vesicle, or a community currency, the first declaration is the same: what is kept in, and who is inside.

### 1.5 What cannot be seen from here

Indeterminacy is the least observable state of the path. It is kept in the grammar because the path needs a before; nothing is claimed about it that a case could test.

Many boundaries are graded rather than sharp: the edge between forest and grassland, a currency accepted in some shops and not others. The grammar allows boundaries of degree. How degree is measured belongs to each realisation.

Some boundaries are drawn by an observer for a purpose — the scope of a model, the border of a map. These are boundaries of the observer's system, not of the system observed. Confusing the two is among the commonest errors in any description.

### 1.6 Objection and reply

*Objection.* Spencer-Brown's calculus begins from a single act — draw a distinction — which makes a boundary and an indication at once. Separating them multiplies entities without need.

*Reply.* The separation earns its place only if separation without identification occurs. The membrane before the cell and the circle of acceptance before the currency are such cases. If, across a fair record of system origins, boundaries did not form before identities, the single act would be the better account, and Parts 1 and 2 should be one. That is a claim about the world, and it is held in the register on those terms.

## Part 2 — Distinction

### 2.1 The state

Within the boundary, difference begins to hold. This, not that. Here, not there. A distinction is a difference that holds and can be found again: what was separated is now a *something*, and can be re-identified across occasions. This is the origin of system in the full sense.

Distinction in a system and recognition by an observer are two events. Gravity distinguished masses from one another for billions of years before it was named. When it was named, an observing system — the people who named it — took a step on its own path. Gravity's path did not change.

So it is that what is not recorded remains hidden and what is recorded becomes visible. For a distinction that operates whether or not it is recorded, recording changes its standing — what can be known, cited, governed — and not its operation. For a distinction made by the act of recording — a coin declared legal tender, a contract signed, a share issued — recording is the distinction. The first kind is representative. The second is constitutive. Both are real, and they are different.

### 2.2 Through the four dimensions

**Interpretation** arises here. The system is now describable: what it is, what it is not, what counts within it. Meaning begins as the holding of a difference.

**Structure** deepens. With distinction, the interior can have parts, and parts can have relations. The form given at the boundary becomes an organisation.

**Regime**, seen from outside, asks what the distinction permits and forbids. A distinction drawn is already a rule in waiting: what is marked as one kind will be treated as that kind.

**Realisation**, seen from outside, is re-identification in practice: the system finding the same thing again, and acting on what it finds.

### 2.3 Why it cannot be removed or merged

Merged into boundary, distinction could not explain identity through replacement. A cell replaces most of its molecules; a currency replaces its notes. They remain the same cell and the same currency not because the same material stays inside the same boundary, but because a distinction holds through the replacement.

Merged into possibility, distinction would leave nothing for the options to be options *of*. A space of outcomes needs named things for the outcomes to happen to. Every variable must be named before it can be given a range. A description whose distinctions are left implicit cannot be checked, because no one can say what its terms refer to.

### 2.4 Two systems

**The cell.** The cells of a body carry markers by which the immune system tells self from non-self. The distinction is constitutive for the body's response: what is marked as non-self is attacked. Autoimmune disease is a distinction drawn in the wrong place, and everything downstream inherits the error.

**The currency.** The token acquires a mark, an issuer, a unit, and in time a law. Declaring it legal tender makes it so within a jurisdiction. Distinction is also what makes the currency countable: one unit, not a heap of metal. A forged note accepted as genuine is a distinction failing — and the failure is found, when it is found, by tracing back to the mark.

### 2.5 What cannot be seen from here

Some distinctions have no sharp edge: species, languages, the moment a growing thing becomes an organism. The grammar allows distinction by degree and by resemblance. It does not claim that every distinction is crisp.

The same material can be distinguished in many ways, each valid for some purpose. The grammar does not say which distinction is right. It says that whichever is drawn determines everything that follows from it, and should therefore be declared.

### 2.6 Objection and reply

*Objection.* If recognition can come long after distinction, the claim that "what is not recorded remains hidden" is either trivial or false. Gravity was never hidden from the planets.

*Reply.* Hidden from the record, not from the world. The claim is about standing, not operation: until it is recorded, a representative distinction cannot be cited, taught, governed or built upon by those who have not recorded it. For a constitutive distinction the claim is stronger: unrecorded, it does not exist at all. An unsigned contract is not a hidden contract. It is not yet a contract.

## Part 3 — Possibility

### 3.1 The state

Once there are things that hold, there is a space of what can become of them. Possibility is that space: the states and outcomes allowed by the distinctions drawn and the constraints in force, before any one of them is realised. It includes what a system represents to itself of what has not yet happened — plans, models, expectations, projections.

Possibility is not probability. Probability is a measure placed on a possibility space; the space comes first. A space may exist with no agreed measure, and an outcome may arrive that no declared space contained.

The space has structure. Some possibilities are open now; some open only after others are realised; some exclude one another. What shapes the space is what is permitted.

### 3.2 Through the four dimensions

**Regime** arises here. Rules shape the space: what is permitted determines which outcomes remain open, and in what order. A possibility space without regime is not a space but a blur.

**Interpretation** is now multiple. The same system can be read toward different futures; each open possibility carries its own meaning.

**Structure** is the shape of the space: its branches, dependencies and exclusions.

**Realisation**, seen from outside, is not yet. Nothing in possibility has happened. But a system that represents its possibilities can act on the representation, and that action is real: a plan changes what is done before what is planned arrives.

### 3.3 Why it cannot be removed or merged

Without possibility, everything that exists would be necessary. There would be no alternative, no choice, no risk, and no account of systems — organisms and institutions alike — that act on representations of futures that have not happened.

Merged into existence, possibility would make the expected and the actual one thing. Every practice that sets a projection against an outcome — budget against result, forecast against observation, expected against actual — depends on keeping the two states apart. The difference between them is where information lives.

### 3.4 Two systems

**The cell.** A genome allows many patterns of expression; which genes are expressed depends on signals and on the cell's present state. Regulation shapes the space: some expressions become possible only after others, and some foreclose others.

**The currency.** A unit can be spent, saved, lent or held. The currency as a whole can inflate, deflate or fail. An issuer's projections are representations of this space with a measure placed on it, and its rules — what it may and may not do — are the regime that shapes the space. A projection's mean is not a prediction that the mean will happen. It is the reference against which what does happen is read.

### 3.5 What cannot be seen from here

A possibility space depends on the distinctions drawn. Two observers with different distinctions see different spaces for the same system. The grammar does not choose between them.

Some outcomes are in no declared space until they happen. The grammar accommodates them only after the fact, by redrawing the space. This is a real limit: possibility cannot be fully generated in advance. A system that continues well is one that records the outcomes its spaces did not contain, rather than redrawing its spaces in silence.

### 3.6 Objection and reply

*Objection.* This is Aristotle's distinction between potentiality and actuality under new names.

*Reply.* Much of the ground is shared, and gladly. The narrower claim here is that the passage from possibility to existence runs through a constraint that selects, that regime is the dimension in which the constraint first arises, and that the passage shows the same four dimensions in every domain — in a cell, a currency, a court, a measurement. That is a claim about structure that Aristotle's distinction does not make, and that a case could refute.

## Part 4 — Existence

### 4.1 The state

From the space of what can be, one possibility is realised. Existence is that realisation: an event or state that has happened, can be recorded, and has consequences. The unit of existence is the event. Before it, something could be; after it, something is so, and the record can say so.

Realisation comes through constraint. A measurement, a decision, a judgment, a signature, a settlement: something selects, and the space collapses to what was selected. Regime, which shaped the space, completes the selection at the crossing into existence. In some domains what selects has no agent, and in some it is disputed; the grammar says only that a selection occurred, not what made it.

### 4.2 Through the four dimensions

**Realisation** arises here. The selected possibility is enacted and has effects. It acts, and is acted upon.

At existence all four dimensions are present together. The system has a singular identity (释): *this* one. It has a coherent form (构). It has defined limits, and where it is an agent, a will within them (度). It has observable effects (行). Lacking identity, it could not be referred to. Lacking form, it would fall apart. Lacking limits, it would be unbounded or inert. Lacking effects, it would be undetectable.

### 4.3 Why it cannot be removed or merged

Merged into possibility, existence would leave nothing settled and nothing to record. Accounting, law and science all depend on the event: the moment after which a thing is so, and is entered.

Merged into continuation, existence could not be told apart from persistence. A single loss, a single judgment, a single division of a cell exists fully without implying that anything will last. Whether it lasts is a further question, with its own conditions.

### 4.4 Two systems

**The cell.** Division realises one outcome of the cell cycle. Before it, a checkpoint must be passed: a constraint that selects. After it there are two cells, each with identity, form, limits and effects. A checkpoint that fails to select is how uncontrolled growth begins, and tracing a tumour back often leads to such a failure.

**The currency.** A coin is minted; a note is issued; a payment settles. Settlement is realisation by constraint in its clearest form. Before it, a payment is a possibility; after it, the payment has happened and neither party alone can undo it.

**The same event at several levels.** A storm damages a building. One physical event happens. It is then realised again at each level that takes it up: as a claim under a contract, as a reserve in an insurer's accounts, as a departure from plan in the financial result. Each is an existence in a different system, with its own four dimensions. The levels do not collapse. This is the recursion of Part 0 in a single afternoon.

### 4.5 What cannot be seen from here

The grammar does not settle what selects in a physical measurement. Interpretations of physics differ on it, and realisation by constraint should not be read as taking a side.

Some things exist in some dimensions and not others: a contested law, a disputed debt, a rumoured event. The grammar describes these as partial presence. Whether partial presence at formation foretells failure is a claim about the world, and it can be tested only by recording the presence before the failure.

### 4.6 Objection and reply

*Objection.* Every example of a system with all four dimensions turns out to have existed, and every example lacking one turns out to have failed. The fit is guaranteed by choosing the examples after the fact.

*Reply.* That is true of any scoring made after the outcome, and such scoring is no evidence. At existence, the presence of all four dimensions is a matter of definition: something that has happened has identity, form, limits and effects, or it has not happened. What the definition cannot do is predict. Whether a dimension weakly present at formation foretells an early end is a separate claim, which only a record made at formation, and read later, can test. The register holds it on those terms.

## Part 5 — Continuation

### 5.1 The state

What exists begins at once to degrade. Material wears; relationships loosen; meaning fades; conditions change. Existence that is not renewed ends. Continuation is existence renewed across time.

What persists is the system's form, its Being. What is renewed is its State: its present configuration, at every moment. A system continues not by staying the same but by regenerating its four dimensions faster than they decay. Gold persists as gold while a ring is worn thin and reforged; an organism persists while its cells are replaced; a nation persists through changes of government.

Renewal is continuous, so the system at one moment is not the same instance as at the next. An education system is not the same tomorrow if teachers enter or leave; a body is not the same matter from one day to the next; in a strict reading, yesterday's self is gone. Whether the renewed system is still the same system is not a fact found in it but a judgement of the describing agent: a boundary drawn in time and fixed by recognition. Whether the judgement is literally true of persons is left to philosophy. Here it is a rule of description: a system continues while its form is renewed and recognised as the same.

Continuation is where the path returns to itself. Here all four dimensions, which arose one by one, must be renewed together, and the failure of any one is enough.

### 5.2 Through the four dimensions

**Interpretation** is renewed as narrative: why this system persists in this state. When the narrative fails, purpose is lost and members disengage, even where structure remains.

**Structure** is renewed by rebuilding what decays: repair, replacement, reorganisation. When renewal stops, capability is lost.

**Regime** is renewed through triggers: a variable is watched, a threshold is set, and crossing the threshold forces renewal before collapse. Without triggers, decay is slow, unseen, and ends suddenly.

**Realisation** is renewed by adaptation: operating in conditions that have changed. Without adaptation, the state becomes unfit for the world it is in.

### 5.3 Why it cannot be removed or merged

Without continuation, every system would be a single event, and identity through replacement — the same organism with new molecules, the same institution with new members, the same currency with new notes — could not be described.

Merged into existence, continuation would erase the difference between being and lasting, and with it the question every designer, keeper and governor of a system actually faces: not whether it exists, but whether it will still exist, and in what state.

### 5.4 Two systems

**The cell.** Homeostasis is continuation. The genome stands as narrative; proteins and membranes are turned over and repaired; set points and feedback trigger correction when temperature, acidity or sugar drift; expression changes as conditions change. Illness, traced back, is very often a trigger that did not fire or a renewal that did not come.

**The currency.** A currency continues while trust in it is renewed: by a record of being accepted, by the replacement of worn notes and the upkeep of payment systems, by rules that trigger action when prices drift, and by adaptation to new ways of paying. A currency whose triggers have no enforced response — prices drifting and nothing done — is how hyperinflation begins. The mechanism of this renewal between agents — how contribution, signals, expectation and trust become credibility and credit across time — is the subject of the Trust Framework, a realisation of this architecture at the level of cooperation.

### 5.5 What cannot be seen from here

The systems that are visible to us across long spans of time are visible because they lasted. Failures are recorded less, and remembered less. Any sample of the past is tilted toward what continued, and any lesson drawn from it must allow for the tilt.

Degradation in physical systems is entropy in the strict sense. For meanings and institutions, "entropy" is a likeness for decay without renewal. It is a useful likeness and should not be mistaken for a law.

How long a system lasts as a function of which dimensions it renews well is a question this architecture raises and does not answer. It is a claim about the world, and it belongs in the register.

### 5.6 Objection and reply

*Objection.* "Systems that renew all four dimensions persist" is unfalsifiable: any system that failed can be said, afterwards, to have failed to renew something.

*Reply.* So it can, if the saying is done afterwards. The claim becomes falsifiable when renewal is recorded first: when a system's narrative, structure, triggers and adaptation are assessed by a rule fixed in advance, and its fate is read later. Until such a record exists, the claim is a projection, not a finding. The architecture says so, and keeps the claim where it can be tested.

## Part 6 — The Return

### 6.1 What a system is

A system is what is identified as having traversed the six states and as present in all four dimensions at its present state. A description of a system is whole when it fills the grid for that system with no remainder and no contradiction. Wholeness belongs to the description. Persistence belongs to the system. The two should not be confused.

### 6.2 The theory as a system

The architecture claims to describe any system. It is a system, and so it should be describable in its own terms.

It arose from indeterminacy: many partial frameworks — of trust, of continuation, of coexistence, of governance — before any was seen as a case of something deeper. Its boundary is its scope: any describable system. Its distinctions are its terms: six states, four dimensions. Its possibilities include its own refutation: a system that needs a fifth dimension, a system whose own record shows a state out of order, a round trip that does not return. It exists in its published versions. It continues through versioning, a ledger of what has been admitted, and a register of what is still to be tested.

In the four dimensions: its interpretation is the preface and the definitions; its structure is the grid and its recursion; its regime is the rule of refutation and the separation of grammar from claims about the world; its realisation is in the works that generate, represent and trace with it.

### 6.3 What the return shows, and what it does not

That the theory can describe itself shows that it is consistent with itself. It does not show that it is true. A false theory can describe itself without contradiction. The first line of the preface says as much: every theory rests upon conditions it cannot make clear from within itself.

So the return does not close the theory. The grammar may be complete — no gap in its description of itself — and remain open in everything it says about the world. What closes a claim about the world is outside the theory: cases, tests, and the judgment of others who did not make it.

### 6.4 The round trip

There is one test the theory can run on itself that is more than self-description. Take a work generated from the grammar, or one that realises it; trace it back, without sight of the grammar's declaration, and see whether the trace returns the six states, the four dimensions and the order in which they arise. Where the trace returns, the reversibility of Part 0 holds for that work. Where it does not, the state at which the trace departs is where the grammar must be renewed.

The descent with which this work began was a trace. This Part is the return. Between them is the path, told forward by something that continues.

## The Lineage of the Question

This architecture was reached by descent from its own realisations, without prior study of systems theory, Eastern philosophy or Western ontology. Only afterwards did it become clear how much of its ground had been travelled before, by traditions that did not know of one another.

Laozi's 道生一，一生二，二生三，三生万物 names the generation of the many from the undivided, and the 易传's 原始反终 names the reversal by which the beginning is understood from the end. Aristotle's potentiality and actuality cover much of the passage from possibility to existence. Peirce's categories of quality, fact and law touch possibility, existence and regime. Spencer-Brown's *Laws of Form* founds a calculus on the single act of drawing a distinction. Maturana and Varela's autopoiesis, and Luhmann's systems built on distinction, describe systems that renew themselves. Von Bertalanffy's general systems theory and the cybernetics of feedback describe the triggers of renewal. Turchin's metasystem transition describes how a new level of control arises over existing systems. Pappus described analysis and synthesis; generative grammar and its reversible forms describe a single grammar run both ways.

On the stance of 0.7, Kant's separation of the world from the form of its appearance, Searle's social ontology, Kuhn on paradigms and Popper on refutation are further companions; Heraclitus, the Buddhist doctrine of momentariness, Whitehead and Parfit stand alongside the account of renewal in Part 5.

These traditions are cited as companions on the ground, not as sources, and nothing here asks them to agree with one another or with this work. What this work adds is the separation of boundary from distinction; the arising of the four dimensions at the four crossings of the path; the recursion of State into Being on one grid; and reversibility, with the round trip as the theory's test of itself.
